How far to trust it
  • The ladder numbers stop at Platinum. Win rates come from Untapped.gg's free data, which covers Bronze through Platinum only. Diamond and Mythic are not in it. The top deck already gets weaker from Gold to Platinum, so the order may differ higher up.
  • Tournament results are best-of-three. They come from Magic Online Challenges and Leagues, where sideboards exist. They show what strong players choose, not what wins best-of-one — and the two pictures barely overlap. The most-played ladder deck has zero tournament entries.
  • Tiers are a reading of that evidence, weighted to the ladder because this is a best-of-one report. Game plans and mulligan notes are worked out from the lists and card text; no source publishes them for these builds.
Bans: nothing has changed since Aug 10, 2026. The next Banned & Restricted announcement is Oct 12, 2026, five days after this was written — check it before crafting anything expensive.

What to queue: Mono-White Auras if you want the cheapest strong deck and are below Diamond. Reanimator or Jeskai Artifacts if you would rather beat it. Orzhov Lifegain if you want to play the new cards and still win. Azorius Control only if your queue is full of Auras.

What changed since the Aug 13 report

Seven weeks, one set
  • Neither August top deck leads any more. Mono-Green Landfall and Izzet Spellementals were the S tier then. On the ladder they now sit at 54.1% and 53.2%; in tournaments Landfall fell from 12.0% of winning decks in September to 3.3% of Challenge decks, and Spellementals from 16.5% to 9.8%.
  • Lifegain took the most from Reality Fracture. Ajani Resolute, Liliana the Faultless and Enlightened Confidant went straight into nearly every Orzhov list, and the lifegain family is now 14% of the ladder.
  • Two decks rose without new cards. Jeskai Artifacts and Reanimator were fringe in September (under 3% each); both are now among the best-performing decks because they beat what the format became.
  • Gone: Izzet Prowess with Bilbo, Azorius Tempo and Four-Colour Legends from the August list have no meaningful presence. Izzet Prowess survives as a different build around Pompous Battlemage.

Importing a list

60 cards, no sideboard

Press Copy, then in Arena go to Decks → Import. Every box is a 60-card main deck that was downloaded from its source and counted by script, and checked against the Standard ban list including Leyline of Resonance (banned in Arena best-of-one only).

Lists marked "Arena ladder list" are the most-played version of that deck on the ladder, with no named pilot. Lists from Magic Online were built for best-of-three; they import fine, but their sideboards are on the linked source page. If Arena rejects a card name from a Magic Online list, the card has a different name on Arena.

S

Mono-White Auras

13.3% of ladder · 57.6% wins

Plan: One-mana creatures that grow whenever an enchantment enters (Optimistic Scavenger, Skyward Spider), stacked with Ethereal Armor and Sheltered by Ghosts. Shardmage's Rescue protects the creature you have invested in. Everything costs one or two mana.

Good against: Mono-Green Landfall, Rakdos Lizards, Boros Dwarves, sacrifice decks, Orzhov Lifegain.

Struggles against: Azorius Control, Jeskai Artifacts, Golgari removal decks — anything with cheap sweepers or a pile of removal.

Keep / mulligan: 20 lands. Keep two lands with a one-drop creature and at least one Aura. Auras with no creature, or creatures with no Aura, is a mulligan. Save Shardmage's Rescue for the turn the opponent has removal mana up.

Reality Fracture cards: None. The best best-of-one deck in the data did not change with the set.

This is a best-of-one deck only: it has no entries in eight days of Magic Online tournaments. Its win rate also falls from 57.2% in Gold to 55.6% in Platinum, and there is no public data above that, so expect it to be weaker at Diamond and Mythic.

Reanimator (Sultai)

5.0% of ladder · 58.7% wins

Plan: Fill the graveyard with Cache Grab, Overlord of the Balemurk and Formidable Speaker, then cast Superior Spider-Man as a copy of the biggest creature there — ideally Bringer of the Last Gift. Terror of the Peaks and Deceit are the fair backup plan.

Good against: Control of every kind, Jeskai Artifacts, Boros Superfriends, Izzet Spellementals.

Struggles against: Fast sacrifice and Lizard decks; slightly behind against Mono-White Auras.

Keep / mulligan: 24 lands across three or four colours. Keep hands with a turn-two or turn-three enabler and the colours to cast it. A hand of expensive creatures with no way to discard or mill them is a mulligan. Nobody plays graveyard hate in game one of best-of-one, which is much of why this deck does so well here.

Reality Fracture cards: Seasoned Cryomancer in most tournament lists (draw two, discard two, tap blockers). The most-played ladder list does not run it.

Jeskai Artifacts

3.9% of ladder · 57.3% wins

Plan: Simulacrum Synthesizer makes a Construct each time a three-mana-or-more artifact enters; United Battlefront and Repurposing Bay keep them coming. The interaction is all artifacts too: Pinnacle Starcage sweeps away everything costing two or less, and Authority of the Consuls makes opposing creatures enter tapped.

Good against: Mono-White Auras, all three lifegain decks, Boros Superfriends, Boros Dwarves.

Struggles against: Reanimator and Azorius Control. About even with Mono-Green Landfall.

Keep / mulligan: 22 lands. Against the ladder's aggro decks you need an early Pinnacle Starcage, Authority of the Consuls or Sawblades, so keep those. Synthesizer with nothing to trigger it and no early interaction is too slow.

Reality Fracture cards: Essentially none. It rose because it is well placed against what everyone else is playing.

Orzhov Lifegain

5.4% of ladder · 53.9% wins (lifegain family 14.0%)

Plan: Every creature entering gains life (Hinterland Sanctifier, Haliya, Liliana the Faultless), and every life gain pays you: Ajani Resolute gains loyalty, Starscape Cleric and Lunar Convocation drain, Enlightened Confidant draws, and Amalia eventually clears the board.

Good against: Control decks (Azorius, four-colour, Golgari) and Boros Dwarves.

Struggles against: Jeskai Artifacts, Mono-White Auras, Reanimator, Mono-Green Landfall.

Keep / mulligan: 20–22 lands, curve of one to three. Keep two lands with a one-drop life source and a payoff. Hands that are all payoffs with no creatures to trigger them are weak.

Reality Fracture cards: Ajani Resolute, Liliana the Faultless and Enlightened Confidant — four copies of each in the most-played list. No archetype took more from the set.

Tournament players use nearly the same 60: Gxs3v3n's 2nd-place list, Oct 6.

Budget alternative: Mono-White Lifegain, no new cards (6.2% of ladder, 54.4% wins)

Mono-Green Landfall

5.5% of ladder · 54.1% wins

Plan: Llanowar Elves into Icetill Explorer and Earthbender Ascension, with Fabled Passage and Escape Tunnel producing several landfall triggers a turn. Sazh's Chocobo grows per land and Mightform Harmonizer doubles a creature's power for the kill.

Good against: Four-colour control, lifegain decks, Boros Superfriends.

Struggles against: Mono-White Auras, heavily — the most-played deck on the ladder is its worst matchup. Also behind against Rakdos Lizards.

Keep / mulligan: 26 lands. Keep hands with Llanowar Elves or a three-mana engine plus a land that fetches. No landfall creature is a mulligan.

Reality Fracture cards: Hexhaven Invigorator in a minority of lists; most are unchanged.

This is the tournament build. The most-played ladder list under the same name is a cheaper, different deck (Mossborn Hydra, Tifa Lockhart, Ordeal of Nylea), so the ladder win rate above is not a measurement of this exact 60.

Azorius Control (Jace)

3.9% of ladder · 53.7% wins

Plan: Counter and remove (Countersculpt, No More Lies, Get Lost, Day of Judgment) until The Theorist, Jace Beleren sticks; it draws an extra card every opposing draw step and makes blockers. Countersculpt, Fatehold Charm and Jace's Machinations all add loyalty to Jace.

Good against: Mono-White Auras and Mono-White Lifegain, by a wide margin; also Jeskai Artifacts and Boros Superfriends.

Struggles against: Reanimator, Rakdos Lizards, sacrifice decks, Boros Dwarves, Orzhov Lifegain.

Keep / mulligan: 25–26 lands. Keep three or four lands with cheap interaction. Most of the ladder is creature decks, so a hand of counterspells with no removal or sweeper is the risky keep.

Reality Fracture cards: Built on them: The Theorist, Jace Beleren; Countersculpt; Jace's Machinations; Fatehold Charm; Theorist's Sanctum; plus Chandra, Chill of Compliance in the ladder list.

Rakdos Lizards

2.7% of ladder · 53.8% wins

Plan: Cheap Lizards that ping (Hired Claw, Iridescent Vinelasher) switch on the cards that care whether an opponent lost life this turn. Master of Barbs pumps the team whenever an opponent takes noncombat damage; Ingris Stingerquill makes every attacker deal an extra point.

Good against: Azorius Control, Reanimator, Mono-Green Landfall.

Struggles against: Mono-White Auras and Mono-White Lifegain — the two most-played decks on the ladder.

Keep / mulligan: 23 lands, curve stops at three. Keep any two-lander with a one-drop Lizard and a two-drop. Without a play on turn one or two the deck loses the race it is built for.

Reality Fracture cards: Master of Barbs, Ingris Stingerquill and Stingerquill Charm, in nearly every list.

Mono-Red Burn

1.9% of ladder · 54.6% wins

Plan: Twenty Mountains and forty cards costing one or two: hasty creatures (Slickshot Show-Off, Emberheart Challenger, Hired Claw) and burn (Boltwave, Burst Lightning, Lightning Strike, Shock).

Keep / mulligan: Keep two or three lands with at least one creature. All-burn hands are fine on the play.

Reality Fracture cards: None.

Dimir Midrange

1.1% of ladder · 55.8% wins

Plan: Cheap evasive creatures (Spyglass Siren, Floodpits Drowner) connect to enable Kaito, Bane of Nightmares and Enduring Curiosity, backed by one- and two-mana removal and counterspells.

Good against: Slightly ahead against Mono-White Auras on a small sample.

Reality Fracture cards: Yuriko, Hope from the Shadows and Theorix Charm in about half of lists.

Seven Challenge top 8s, second only to Jund Sacrifice — but only about 4,200 ladder matches, so treat the win rate as approximate.

Strong in best-of-three, ordinary here

CTournament decks

These four fill the Magic Online top 8s and each is 1.5% or less of the best-of-one ladder, with win rates a point or two below the decks above. They lean on sideboards, and two of them fold to the ladder's most-played deck or its best one. Play them if you also play best-of-three.

Izzet Spellementals — 9.8% of Challenge decks, 1.5% of ladder (53.2%)

Cantrips fill the graveyard so Hearth Elemental and Eddymurk Crab cost almost nothing, then Sunderflock bounces every non-Elemental. It was the top deck in August and through September; it has lost the most ground since. Good against Auras, very poor against Reanimator. Faria_Limer won the Oct 2 Challenge with this list.

Why best-of-one is its own format

  • No sideboard. Graveyard decks, Aura decks and artifact decks never face the cards that punish them. That is the main reason Reanimator and Mono-White Auras top this list and not the tournament one.
  • Fast, linear decks are over-represented. Most of the ladder is creature decks. Flexible removal earns its slot; narrow answers sit in hand.
  • The hand smoother helps land count, not colours. Arena's best-of-one opening-hand algorithm favours a normal land-to-spell ratio. It will not fix a three-colour mana base, so do not trim lands from Reanimator or control. MTG Arena Zone on hand smoothing.
  • Leyline of Resonance is banned in Arena best-of-one only. A Magic Online list can legally contain it; none of the lists here do.

Gaps — why this is a snapshot

  1. No best-of-one data above Platinum was available. Every ladder number here describes Bronze through Platinum.
  2. No major Standard tournament has been played with the set. Tournament evidence is seven Magic Online Challenges over eight days, with the same pilots appearing repeatedly — both Izzet Prowess wins are one player, as are all three Simic Landfall entries.
  3. Deck names do not always mean the same 60 cards. The ladder's Mono-Green Landfall and Reanimator lists differ from the tournament ones, so a ladder win rate is not a measurement of a tournament list.
  4. Decks under roughly 5,000 ladder matches (Dimir Midrange, Jund Sacrifice, Izzet Spellementals, Boros Dragons) carry a point or two of noise.
  5. MTGGoldfish's headline metagame percentages cover 30 days and are mostly pre-set; they were not used. Shares were recounted from individual events.
  6. Matchup notes give direction only, drawn from ladder results; pairs with few games were left out.
